Privacy Policy

Effective 29 October 2026 · Applies to the Ribbon app for iPhone and this website

The short version. Ribbon keeps everything on your iPhone. There's no account, no server, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and the app never connects to the internet. I (the developer) never receive your reminders or anything else you put into Ribbon, so I can't see, sell or share it. Apple, and features of iOS that you choose to use, may handle some information under Apple's own terms, as explained below.

Who this policy is from

Ribbon is made by Finley David R Eaton, an individual developer based in England ("I", "me"). If you have any question about this policy or your privacy, email CONTACT_EMAIL.

This policy covers the Ribbon iPhone app (including its Lock Screen countdowns and Siri shortcuts) and this website. It explains what information exists, where it's kept, who can see it, and what choices you have.

What I collect

Nothing. Ribbon doesn't collect, transmit or store any personal data on servers controlled by me or anyone working for me. The app contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or other third-party code, makes no network requests of its own, and has no account system. Its App Store privacy label is "Data Not Collected".

What Ribbon stores on your iPhone

To work, Ribbon saves the things you enter on your own device. None of this is sent to me.

All of this is kept in a single file in Ribbon's private storage on your iPhone, which other apps can't read. The file is saved with iOS Data Protection at the "Complete" level, which means iOS encrypts it using a key tied to your device passcode and it can't be read while your iPhone is locked. This protection only works if your iPhone has a passcode set.

Ribbon also keeps up to 7 daily copies of that file (and one copy made just before you restore a backup) in the same private, protected storage, so a mistake can be undone.

Two small items are kept in Ribbon's standard app preferences, which use iOS's default protection rather than the "Complete" level: the number of the last version whose "What's new" screen you've seen, and a list of countdowns already started automatically (a reminder's internal ID and a time, never its title).

Features that involve iOS or Apple

Some features hand information to iOS, or to Apple, so they can work. These are part of your iPhone, not part of Ribbon, and Apple handles them under the Apple Privacy Policy.

Notifications

Reminders are delivered as local notifications: Ribbon gives iOS the reminder's title (and its notes, or a subscription's cost, only if you turn on "Show notes in notifications") and the time, and iOS shows it then. No push-notification server is involved. Notifications can appear on your Lock Screen, where anyone holding your iPhone can see them. You can hide previews in iPhone Settings → Notifications. iOS features such as notification summaries may process notification text on your device.

Live Activities (Lock Screen countdowns)

When a countdown runs, the reminder's title and due time appear on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, where they can be seen without unlocking.

Face ID and passcode lock

If you turn on the lock, iOS checks your face or passcode and only tells Ribbon whether it succeeded. Ribbon never receives or stores your face data or passcode.

Siri and Shortcuts

Ribbon's shortcuts ("Add to Ribbon", "What's next in Ribbon") run on your iPhone and require Face ID or your passcode. If you use them by speaking to Siri, Apple processes your voice request according to your Siri settings, which may include processing on Apple's servers.

Backups of your iPhone

If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or a computer, iOS includes Ribbon's data in that backup, protected by Apple's backup encryption. Ribbon doesn't upload anything itself.

Things you choose to share

Backup files you save

Settings → Backup → "Save a backup to Files" creates a copy of all your Ribbon data, including reminder titles, notes and any subscription costs. This file isn't encrypted. It goes wherever you choose to save it (for example iCloud Drive, another app, or a USB drive), and whoever can open it can read it. That storage is covered by its own provider's terms. Restoring reads a file you choose and replaces Ribbon's data with it.

Share cards

The weekly recap and Miso's wardrobe can make a picture to share. It shows only counts, days and Miso, never reminder titles, notes or tag names. It's only sent if you choose where to send it, and then it's handled by that app or person.

Information Apple may give me

Apple distributes Ribbon, and may share some limited information with developers under Apple's terms, not mine:

  • App Store: Apple gives developers anonymised, combined statistics, such as download numbers. If you've chosen to share analytics with app developers (iPhone Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements), Apple may also share crash reports and usage figures that don't identify you.
  • TestFlight (beta testers only): if you test a pre-release version, Apple shares your name and email address if I invited you directly, plus crash reports, device and iOS version, and any feedback or screenshots you choose to send. I use these only to fix problems with Ribbon, and delete them when they're no longer needed or when you ask me to.
  • Email: if you email me, I'll see your email address and whatever you write. I'll use it only to reply and to improve Ribbon, and delete it when it's no longer needed or when you ask.

I don't receive your Apple Account details, payment information or location from Apple.

This website

This website is hosted on GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies and uses no analytics, ads, web fonts or other third-party scripts. The Help & FAQ, Privacy Policy and Terms of Use links in Ribbon's Settings → About open this website in Safari. Ribbon itself doesn't load it. GitHub, as the host, may log technical information such as your IP address to keep the service running and secure. See the GitHub Privacy Statement.

What I never do

  • Sell, rent or trade your information.
  • Track you across other apps or websites, or use advertising identifiers.
  • Connect to your bank, read your App Store purchases, or cancel subscriptions for you. Subscription details are typed in by you and stay on your iPhone.
  • Read your Apple Reminders, Calendar, contacts, photos or location. Ribbon doesn't ask for access to any of them.

How long information is kept, and deleting it

Your data stays on your iPhone until you delete it. Deleting a reminder removes it from the app, and it drops out of the daily copies within 7 days. The copy made just before a restore is replaced the next time you restore. Deleting the Ribbon app deletes all of its data and copies from your iPhone permanently, except copies inside your own iPhone backups or backup files you've saved. Those are kept and deleted under your control.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law (the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018), and similar laws elsewhere, you have rights over your personal data, including to access, correct, delete or move it. Because I never receive the data in Ribbon, you exercise these rights directly in the app: you can view, edit, export (Backup) and delete everything yourself. For anything I do hold (for example, emails you've sent me or TestFlight feedback), contact me at CONTACT_EMAIL. If you're unhappy with how your data has been handled, you can complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk, or to your local data protection authority.

Children

Ribbon is suitable for all ages and doesn't knowingly collect personal data from anyone, including children under 13 (or the equivalent minimum age where you live), because it doesn't collect personal data at all.

Security

Ribbon relies on iOS's built-in protections: app sandboxing, Data Protection encryption and optional Face ID or passcode lock. No method of storage is completely secure. Your data is safest with a strong iPhone passcode, and if you keep any backup file you save somewhere safe.

Changes to this policy

If Ribbon ever changes in a way that affects your privacy (for example, adding an optional sync feature), I'll update this policy before that version is released, change the effective date above, and mention it in the app's "What's new" screen. Anything that would send data off your iPhone would be optional and explained first.

That includes someone else accessing your unlocked iPhone, notifications or countdowns shown on your Lock Screen, backup files you save or share, your iPhone backups, or the services of Apple and other third parties.
